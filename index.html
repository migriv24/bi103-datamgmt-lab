Ethics, Bias & Peer Review

The assignment asks how these concepts relate to personal body data collection. The honest answer is that they are all tangled together, and this dataset makes that pretty visible.

⚖

Ethical Considerations

I use Samsung Health and MyFitnessPal knowing both companies profit from user data. I disagree with this in principle but use them anyway because they work and accessible alternatives are limited. That is not a personal failing, it is a structural problem with how health tech is built.

The ethical issues that actually bother me are more specific. I had to sign up for a paid trial just to export my own health history from MyFitnessPal. Samsung's export button for Health data is broken. If data ownership is real, accessing your own records should not require a subscription. That is a consent problem disguised as a UX problem.

In a study context, the key ethical requirements would be: the right to withdraw data at any time, anonymization that actually holds up (re-identification through cross-referencing "anonymous" data is well-documented), and a clear answer to who benefits from the data being collected. I would share my health data freely if I knew it was going toward research rather than ad targeting.

⚖

Reducing Bias

The gaps in this dataset are the most obvious source of bias. The three-month blackout over winter 2024-2025 was not random. It happened during a stressful period when I was not seeing the progress I wanted. If this data were part of a study, that pattern would skew any conclusions about my typical behavior, because I only logged consistently during the periods when things were going better.

Automated data collection from my Galaxy Watch helps with this. The watch records activity whether or not I am motivated to open an app. That is why the Samsung Health data is more complete than the MyFitnessPal logs. The runs from April and May 2026 are there even on the weeks when I would not have thought to log them manually.

Reducing bias in future data collection would mean: consistent weigh-ins at the same time under the same conditions, not selectively logging meals, and treating bad weeks as worth recording just as much as good ones. The data should reflect reality, not a highlight reel.

⚖

Peer Review

I had not looked at my running pace data until building this site. I genuinely did not know if I was improving or staying flat. That is exactly what peer review catches: things you cannot see because you are too close to the data, or because you were not asking the right questions.

A peer reviewing this dataset might point out that the April 2025 weight spike (219 to 230 lbs in two days) is almost certainly water retention and should not be interpreted as a failure. They might also ask why my weight has drifted back up to 218 lbs in spring 2026 despite running more than at any previous point in this dataset. That question does not have an obvious answer in the data. It would require looking at factors like diet and stress that I did not track consistently.

Peer review in personal data collection is not about catching dishonesty. It is about having someone ask the questions you did not think to ask, and notice patterns you normalized because you lived through them.
